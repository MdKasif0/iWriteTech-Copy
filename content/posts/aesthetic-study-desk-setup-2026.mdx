## What a study desk actually needs (and what it doesn't)

Before the product list: most student desks fail for one of two reasons — 
either they're too bare (hard surface, single lamp, phone as a distraction 
within arm's reach) or too cluttered (stationery everywhere, cables across 
the surface, no clear "this is where I study" visual signal). The setup 
below threads that line.

You need: focused lighting, a clear surface, something to keep stationery 
off the desk itself, a proper laptop angle, and cables hidden. You don't 
need a monitor arm, a mechanical keyboard, or any gadget that costs more 
than a textbook.

## How to actually set this up

The order matters more than people think. Do it this way:

1. **Desk mat first** — lay it down so you know the boundaries of your surface
2. **Cable tray under the desk** — clamp it, route all charging cables up through the back edge of the mat, hide the power strip below completely
3. **Laptop stand** — position it toward the back-centre of the mat
4. **Desk organiser** — place to your non-dominant hand side, within easy reach but not between you and the laptop
5. **Lamp** — to your dominant-hand side at the back corner, angled toward the work area not the screen

Everything except the laptop itself now lives in an assigned position. When you sit down, nothing needs to be moved before you can start working — which sounds trivial but is actually one of the biggest practical differences between a desk that gets used and one that becomes a surface for storing things.
